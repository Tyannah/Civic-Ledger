----------
CivicLedger
----------
> Understand who's responsible for a public road, what it was budgeted to cost, whether it was actually delivered — and where to take it if it wasn't.
Built for the OSF × Andela Invention Sprint (Transparency & Accountability track), inspired by OSF's Transformative Peace in Africa: Shifting Power to Communities initiative.
 Link to Deployed App: https://civic-ledger-beige.vercel.app/

-------------
The problem 
-------------
Countries like Uganda, Kenya, and Nigeria have more than one body that is responsible for road construction, maintenance, and planning. Most residents in these countries are unable to pinpoint which of these bodies is responsible for the road they use everyday in the even they want to hold it accountable and report any discrepancies. They also want to know the plans the bodies have for maintenance or completion of the road
In addition, budget documents rarely connect to a project a resident can recognize, and even where a real, free complaints channel exists (Kenya's Commission on Administrative Justice, Uganda's Inspectorate of Government, Nigeria's Public Complaints Commission), most residents don't know it exists or which agency to name.

----------------
What CivicLedger does
----------------
Five steps, 
Choose your Country
Find your road — pick a sample project 
Who's responsible, and who pays — the correct agency for that road class in that country, its mandate, and the oversight/funding structure
Budget, and how it compares — cost-per-km converted to a common benchmark, flagged against typical ranges for that road class
Reporting—  Contact details for the responsible bodies, an auto-generates a complaint pre-filled with the project's facts, routed to the correct real complaints body for that country, with working contact details  
 
---------------
Roadmap, Next Steps
---------------
Add Hospitals, Technical facilities and Water Irrigation checks as well
WhatsApp front end: the dominant messaging channel across all three countries, needs no app install and works on 2G; same underlying logic behind a conversational interface, reachable by anyone with a phone number
Live data ingestion: replacing the curated demo dataset with direct pulls from KeRRA/KURA/KRB, Uganda's Ministry of Works and Transport, and FERMA/state/LGA disclosures
Cross-border corridor comparison: Kenya and Uganda co-fund shared road corridors (e.g. Kapchorwa–Suam / Suam–Endebess–Kitale–Eldoret) — a capability only a multi-country tool can offer
Disclosure on AI tool use
AI development tools were used to help build this prototype (data structuring, front-end implementation, drafting support). The core idea — combining institutional classification, budget benchmarking, community verification, and complaint routing into one cross-country tool, grounded in specific, researched gaps in Kenya's, Uganda's, and Nigeria's accountability structures is the author's own.
